## Narration
A three-digit number decides how much your car, your apartment, and even your phone plan cost you. It's your credit score, and here's how it actually works. In the US, the most common score, FICO, runs from 300 to 850. It's built from five parts. The biggest, about 35 percent, is payment history: do you pay on time? One payment more than 30 days late can hurt for years. Next, about 30 percent, is how much of your available credit you're using. Using a little is good. Maxing out your cards is not. Then, 15 percent is how long you've had credit, 10 percent is new credit you've applied for recently, and 10 percent is your mix, like a card plus a loan. Think of it like a report card that lenders read before they trust you. Higher score, lower interest rates. The fastest wins? Pay on time, every time, and keep your balances low. This isn't financial advice, just how the system works. Follow White Bucket for more.

## Facts to double-check
- FICO scores range from 300 to 850. Source: myFICO.com.
- FICO weights: payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. Source: myFICO.com "What's in my FICO Scores".
- Payments are generally reported late once 30 or more days past due, and late payments can stay on a credit report for up to 7 years. Source: Experian / CFPB.
