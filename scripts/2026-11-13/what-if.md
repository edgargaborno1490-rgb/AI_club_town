## Narration
What if Earth suddenly stopped spinning? Right now, if you're near the equator, you're moving at about 1,670 kilometers per hour. You don't feel it, because everything around you moves too. But if the planet stopped, you wouldn't. You, the air, the oceans, the buildings, everything not bolted to bedrock would keep flying east at that speed. Winds faster than any hurricane ever recorded would scour the ground. Giant waves would race across every ocean. And that's just the first hour. Then it gets stranger. Earth's spin pushes water toward the equator. Without it, the oceans would slowly drain toward the poles, and a huge new continent would rise around the middle of the planet. And one day would last an entire year: six months of blazing sunlight on one side, six months of freezing darkness on the other. The good news? Earth's spin is slowing down, but only by a couple of milliseconds per century. So enjoy your 24-hour days. Follow White Bucket for more.

## Facts to double-check
- Earth's surface speed at the equator is about 1,670 km/h (about 1,040 mph). Source: NASA.
- Rotation pushes water toward the equator (the equatorial bulge); without it, oceans would move toward the poles and an equatorial land belt would appear. Source: Esri / Witold Fraczek modeling, widely reported.
- With no rotation, a solar day would equal one year. Source: basic astronomy.
- The day is lengthening by roughly 1.7–2.3 milliseconds per century. Source: tidal-braking studies (e.g. Stephenson et al., Royal Society).
