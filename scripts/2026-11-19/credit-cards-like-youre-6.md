## Fact notes

- **About twenty-two percent a year:** the average interest rate on credit card accounts that were charged interest was 22.36% (Q3 2026), Federal Reserve G.19 Consumer Credit release, as of Nov 6, 2026: https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/ (FRED series: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TERMCBCCINTNS). An average at commercial banks, not any one person's rate, so the narration says "the average card".
- **"Give ten back next week… and it's free":** most cards give a grace period on purchases: pay the full statement balance by the due date and there's no interest on those purchases (not required by law; cash advances usually charge interest right away). CFPB, as of Nov 19, 2026: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-grace-period-en-47/
- **"Most cookies earn one percent, restaurant cookies three percent":** a common tiered cash-back setup, e.g. 3% on dining and entertainment and 1% everywhere else (Capital One, "How do credit card rewards work?", as of Nov 19, 2026): https://www.capitalone.com/learn-grow/money-management/how-do-credit-card-rewards-work . Rates differ by card, some cap the bonus categories; the narration says "can earn".
- **"Five hundred cookies → five back; all restaurant → fifteen":** the real math: $500 × 1% = $5; $500 × 3% = $15 (per year of $500 in purchases).
- **Cash back or travel points:** points cards earn points per dollar, redeemable for statement credit, gift cards or travel (Chase, "What are credit card points", as of Nov 19, 2026): https://www.chase.com/personal/credit-cards/education/rewards-benefits/what-are-credit-card-points-and-how-do-they-work
- **"Way more than the rewards":** the CFPB found people who carry a balance often pay far more in interest and fees than they earn in rewards (Credit Card Rewards issue spotlight, May 2024, as of Nov 19, 2026): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/issue-spotlight-credit-card-rewards/ . 22% a year vs 1–3% back.
