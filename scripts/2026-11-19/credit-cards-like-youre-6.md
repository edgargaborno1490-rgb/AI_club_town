## Fact notes

- **About twenty-two percent a year:** the average interest rate on credit card accounts that were charged interest was 22.36% (Q3 2026), Federal Reserve G.19 Consumer Credit release, as of Nov 6, 2026: https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/ (series on FRED: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TERMCBCCINTNS). Across all accounts it was about 21.2%. This is an average at commercial banks, not any one person's rate, so the narration says "the average card".
- **"Pay back by the due date and it's free":** most cards give a grace period on purchases: pay the full statement balance by the due date and there's no interest on those purchases. Not required by law, cash advances usually charge interest right away, and paying only part can cost the grace period for the next month too. CFPB, as of Nov 19, 2026: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-grace-period-en-47/
- **"Find your card's rate on your statement":** card statements list the APR (CFPB, "What is APR?"): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-apr-en-47/ (as of Nov 19, 2026).
- **Island:** 49 followers = 49 birds (YouTube 25, TikTok 17, Instagram 4, Facebook 3), town.json as of Nov 19, 2026. "To announce" in island-news.md is empty.
- **Points: "five hundred cookies → five or ten back" = 1–2%:** common flat-rate cash-back cards pay about 1–2% of spending; Bankrate calls a no-fee 2% card "surprisingly hard to beat for most people" (as of Nov 19, 2026): https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/news/cfpb-report-credit-card-rewards-frustration . Points are paid on purchases whether or not you pay on time; "he trusts you" is the kid picture for keeping the card open and in good standing.
- **"Way more than the points":** the CFPB found that people who carry a balance often pay far more in interest and fees than they earn in rewards (Credit Card Rewards issue spotlight, May 2024, as of Nov 19, 2026): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/issue-spotlight-credit-card-rewards/ . 22% a year vs 1–2% back.
