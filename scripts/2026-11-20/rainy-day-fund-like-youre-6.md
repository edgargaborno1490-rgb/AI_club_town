## Narration (record as one paragraph)

How do you build a rainy-day fund? Explained like you're six. … Imagine a piggy bank that's only for oops days. A broken toy. A lost shoe. Here's the checklist. One: give it its own piggy bank… not the one you spend from. Two: pick a small goal first… like enough for one oops. Three: every time you get your allowance, a few coins go in first… before anything else. Four: only open it for a real oops… not for candy. That's called an emergency fund. … In grown-up words: as of May twenty twenty-six, sixty-three percent of adults could pay a surprise four-hundred-dollar bill with cash. ## Fact notes

- In the Federal Reserve's Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025 report (published May 13, 2026; survey fielded October 2025), 63% of adults said they would cover a $400 emergency expense with cash or its equivalent (cash, savings, or a card paid off at the next statement), unchanged from the year before. As of Nov 20, 2026. Sources: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20260513a.htm and https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/63-americans-could-cover-400-184456469.html
- "More than one in three couldn't" (pay with cash): the other 37% would borrow, carry it on a card, or couldn't pay right now (12% couldn't pay at all). As of Nov 20, 2026. Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-savings-investments.htm
- The checklist steps (a separate place for the money, a small goal to start, save automatically / first, use it only for real emergencies) follow the CFPB's emergency fund guide and common bank guidance. As of Nov 20, 2026. Sources: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/ and https://www.capitalone.com/learn-grow/money-management/how-to-build-and-use-emergency-funds/
