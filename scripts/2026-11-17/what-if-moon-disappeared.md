Our island just turned Hawaiian! Palm trees, flower bushes, a sandy beach, a lagoon… and a volcano across the water. It even follows real Hawaii time, so at night the stars, the moon and the tiki torches come out. WhiteBucket moved in and waves from the plaza. The birdhouses are finished, every bucket has a wooden name sign, and workers are building a bird bath… a viewer's idea. Forty-nine birds… one more and we hit fifty! And the next-build plot is open, so comment what we should build. Now… today's question. What if the Moon just disappeared? First, nights get much darker. Some corals wait for moonlight to know when to spawn… and that signal would be gone. Next, the oceans. The Moon makes most of our tides. The Sun makes some too, so tides wouldn't stop… but they'd shrink to about a third of their size. But the biggest change is slow. Earth leans over, about twenty-three degrees. That lean gives us seasons. Right now it only rocks a little, between twenty-two and twenty-four and a half degrees, and the Moon helps hold it steady. Without it, over hundreds of thousands of years, the lean could swing wildly. Scientists still argue how far. Bigger swings mean wild seasons and ice ages. The good news? The Moon isn't going anywhere. It drifts away just under four centimeters a year. See you tomorrow for another island update!

## Fact notes

- **Island (row 0):** facts from AI Club Town `town.json` and `village/island-news.md` as of Nov 17, 2026: Aloha Island look (palms, flower bushes, sandy beach, lagoon, volcano, tiki torches), sky follows real Hawaii time, WhiteBucket waving in the plaza, birdhouses finished, wooden name signs, bird bath being built (YouTube viewer @dakotaboyd4809's idea), 49 birds (YouTube 25, TikTok 17, Instagram 4, Facebook 3), "? NEXT BUILD" plot open. Never show or say the island's web address.
- Tides: the Moon is the main cause of Earth's tides; the Sun's tide-generating force is about half the Moon's. As of Nov 17, 2026. Source: NOAA, https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial_tides/tides02_cause.html (checked: https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-earthscience/chapter/tides/)
- "About a third of their size": NASA JPL scientist Matt Siegler's estimate that tides without the Moon would be about a third of their current range (the Sun's tides remain). As of Nov 17, 2026. Source: https://www.popsci.com/what-would-happen-if-moon-suddenly-disappeared (checked: https://www.astronomy.com/science/what-if-the-moon-disappeared-tomorrow-2/)
- Coral spawning and moonlight: a 2015 study (Kaniewska et al., eLife) found moonlight is an important cue for the timing of mass coral spawning in *Acropora millepora* from the Great Barrier Reef; mass spawning happens a few nights after the full moon in late spring. Narration says "some corals". As of Nov 17, 2026. Sources: https://elifesciences.org/articles/09991 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4721961
- Earth's tilt is about 23.4°; over the last million years it has varied only between 22.1° and 24.5° (a cycle of about 41,000 years). Larger tilt = more extreme seasons. As of Nov 17, 2026. Source: NASA, https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/earth-science/climate-science/milankovitch-orbital-cycles-and-their-role-in-earths-climate/
- Without the Moon, Earth's tilt could vary chaotically: Laskar, Joutel & Robutel (Nature, 1993) found a chaotic zone from near 0° to about 85°; later studies (Lissauer et al. 2012; Li & Batygin 2014) found smaller swings (roughly 0–45° over billions of years). That's why the narration says "scientists still argue how far". Changes would unfold over hundreds of thousands of years, not overnight (Siegler, Popular Science). As of Nov 17, 2026. Sources: https://doi.org/10.1038/361615a0, https://perso.imcce.fr/jacques-laskar/en/general-audience/earth-without-the-moon, https://www.popsci.com/what-would-happen-if-moon-suddenly-disappeared
- The Moon drifts away from Earth about 3.8 cm (1.5 in) a year, measured by bouncing lasers off reflectors left by Apollo astronauts. As of Nov 17, 2026. Source: NASA, https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/ (checked: https://www.astronomy.com/science/what-if-the-moon-disappeared-tomorrow-2/)
- There's no known way for the Moon to vanish; this is a thought experiment. Keep "The Moon isn't going anywhere."
- Bonus (not in the narration): the length of the day wouldn't change right away; the Moon's tides slowly brake Earth's spin, so without it days would stop lengthening (DLR, via Science in School: https://www.scienceinschool.org/article/2013/moon-2).
- Photos: NASA Moon and Apollo laser-reflector images are public domain (NASA media guidelines). Coral spawning: pick a Wikimedia Commons photo with a CC license and put its credit on screen.
