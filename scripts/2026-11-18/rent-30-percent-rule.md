## Narration (record as one paragraph)

The thirty percent rent rule… didn't start as budget advice. It started as a law. In nineteen sixty-nine, Congress capped rent in public housing at twenty-five percent of a family's income. In nineteen eighty-one, that cap went up to thirty. Then it became everyone's rule of thumb. Today, the government calls you "cost burdened" if rent takes more than thirty percent. And in twenty twenty-four… twenty-two point seven million renter households were over that line. That's forty-nine percent… almost half of all renters, a new record. ## Fact notes

- 1969: the Brooke Amendment (Housing and Urban Development Act of 1969, sec. 213(a)) capped rent in public housing at 25% of a tenant's income; in 1981 the cap was raised to 30%, and the 30% benchmark became the common rule of thumb. As of Nov 18, 2026. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke_Amendment and https://www.huduser.gov/portal/node/5796 (checked: https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/AG-2025/1-8_A-Brief-Historical-Overview-of-Affordable.pdf; the 1981 rise came with the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1981, which raised tenant rent contributions: https://www.gao.gov/products/ced-82-116)
- HUD defines cost-burdened households as those paying more than 30% of income for housing (severely burdened: more than 50%). As of Nov 18, 2026. Sources: https://www.huduser.gov/portal/node/5796 and https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/state-nations-housing-2026
- In 2024 (the newest data, in Harvard JCHS's State of the Nation's Housing 2026, released June 17, 2026), a record 22.7 million renter households, 49% of renters, were cost burdened; 12.1 million (26%) were severely burdened. As of Nov 18, 2026. Sources: https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/press-releases/high-costs-and-slumping-demand-squeeze-housing-affordable-units-remain-short-supply and https://www.gsd.harvard.edu/2026/06/state-of-the-nations-housing-2026-finds-affordability-challenges/ (checked: https://www.novoco.com/notes-from-novogradac/harvard-jchs-state-of-the-nations-2026-housing-report-highlights-ongoing-housing-affordability-pressures)
