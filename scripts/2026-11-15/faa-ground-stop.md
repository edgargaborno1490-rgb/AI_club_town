## Narration (record as one paragraph)

On Friday, every plane in America was told to stay on the ground. Here's what happened. At three fifty-four p.m. Eastern, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop. That means no flight could take off, anywhere in the country. Planes already in the air kept flying… and over three thousand landed safely. So why stop everything? Air traffic controllers guide pilots by radio, and those radios run through a private communications network. On Friday, parts of that network started failing. Frequencies cut in and out, and controllers had to jump to backup systems. When controllers can't count on talking to pilots, the safest move is simple… stop sending new planes up. The stop lasted more than four hours and was lifted at eight fifteen p.m. But the delays rolled into the weekend. Thousands of flights were canceled Friday, and more than twenty-five hundred more on Saturday. Officials say there's no sign of a cyberattack. Early reports point to old equipment, but the exact cause is still being investigated. As of November fifteenth, the FAA expects flights to be back to normal by the end of Sunday. ## Fact notes

- The FAA ordered the nationwide ground stop at 3:54 p.m. Eastern (20:54 UTC) on Friday, Nov 13, 2026, and lifted it at 8:15 p.m. Eastern; reports describe more than four hours of disruption. As of Nov 15, 2026. Sources: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/faa-ground-stop-lifted-why-were-us-flights-grounded-and-whats-next and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FAA_nationwide_ground_stop
- A ground stop halts departures; flights already in the air continue. More than 3,000 aircraft landed safely during the disruption (Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin). As of Nov 15, 2026. Sources: https://www.securityweek.com/no-signs-of-hacking-in-faa-ground-stop-as-fbi-reportedly-probes-software-update/ and https://www.flightradar24.com/blog/live/nationwide-ground-stop-issued-for-united-states/
- The FAA cited a telecommunications/communications outage; facilities reported radio frequencies "going in and out", forcing controllers to switch between primary and backup systems. As of Nov 15, 2026. Sources: https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/14/politics/faa-ground-stop-what-we-know and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FAA_nationwide_ground_stop
- Cause not final: officials blamed overloaded routers and switches / equipment failure (CBS reported older routers made 2001–2006); SecurityWeek reported a software update was also being looked at. The narration only says "early reports point to old equipment… still being investigated". As of Nov 15, 2026. Sources: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/faa-issues-nationwide-ground-stop/ and https://www.securityweek.com/no-signs-of-hacking-in-faa-ground-stop-as-fbi-reportedly-probes-software-update/
- No sign of a cyberattack or deliberate interference ("no indication, as of yet"; the White House said no indication of a "nefarious act"). As of Nov 15, 2026. Sources: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/faa-issues-nationwide-ground-stop/ and https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/13/us-grounds-all-flights-after-communication-issue
